# UI/UX Specification

## Product Experience

The interface should feel like a professional finance workspace, not an accounting ledger and not a generic BI dashboard.

The primary experience is organized around management questions:

- How are we performing?
- Are we on plan?
- What changed?
- Why did it change?
- Where are we heading?
- What happens if assumptions change?
- What needs management attention?

## Navigation

Suggested primary areas:

1. Overview
2. Actuals
3. Budget
4. Forecast
5. Scenarios
6. Cash
7. KPIs
8. Reports
9. AI Analyst
10. Data & Imports
11. Settings

Advanced areas can be hidden for simpler organizations.

## Dashboard Principles

- Show a small number of high-value metrics first.
- Make comparison periods obvious.
- Distinguish actual, budget, forecast, and scenario values visually and semantically.
- Surface exceptions rather than filling every screen with charts.
- Allow drill-down from KPI → variance → underlying dimension/data.

## Planning UX

Planning screens should support spreadsheet-like efficiency without becoming an uncontrolled spreadsheet. Users need fast data entry, formulas/drivers where supported, validation, versioning, and approval status.

## AI UX

The AI analyst should appear as a finance workspace capability, not as a gimmicky chatbot. Answers should expose the financial basis of conclusions and offer useful follow-up questions or controlled actions.

## Responsive Design

Desktop is important for finance planning work. Mobile should remain useful for executive review, alerts, approvals, and high-level dashboards.

## Accessibility

Use semantic HTML, keyboard-accessible controls, readable contrast, clear focus states, and accessible table/chart alternatives.
